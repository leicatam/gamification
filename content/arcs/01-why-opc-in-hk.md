# Arc 1 — Why an OPC in Hong Kong, Why Now

This arc establishes the thesis. After it, the learner should be able to defend (or rebut) the
claim that *an AI-augmented one-person HK Ltd is the right vehicle for me*.

The tutor adapts depth, vocabulary and examples to the learner's persona. The content below is the
canonical source; the tutor compresses or expands as needed.

---

## Section 1 — The two waves

### 1.1 Aging society, in HK terms

Hong Kong crossed the "super-aged" line (more than 20% of residents over 65) around 2024. By the
mid-2030s it is on track for roughly 30%. Three consequences matter for this learner:

1. **MPF is widely understood to be insufficient.** The Mandatory Provident Fund replaces only a
   fraction of pre-retirement income for most professionals. Supplemental income matters.
2. **Statutory retirement is fluid.** Many HK employers continue to set retirement at 60 or 65,
   but professionals routinely keep earning past that age — through contracts, advisory roles, or
   their own company.
3. **A large cohort holds expensive expertise.** Finance, logistics, healthcare, education, trade,
   law, engineering — HK's pre-retirement professionals carry decades of know-how that the city
   needs to keep productive.

### 1.2 Agentic AI, in operational terms

A "company" is mostly a coordination structure: someone keeps the books, someone bills clients,
someone drafts contracts, someone answers email, someone files taxes. Agentic AI now performs
draft-quality versions of all of these tasks. That collapses the marginal cost of *running* a
company — even though it does not (and should not) collapse the cost of *deciding* what the
company does.

The interesting consequence is that the smallest unit of viable incorporation has shrunk. A
single human, plus an agent stack, can plausibly run what used to require a small firm.

### 1.3 Where the two waves meet — the OPC

A One Person Corporate, in Hong Kong, is a private company limited by shares (Cap. 622) with one
human director-shareholder. With an AI agent stack handling draft work, the structure can support
several service lines, multiple clients, cross-border invoicing, and clean books — without
employees.

This is not a pitch for everyone to incorporate. It is a pitch for treating the OPC as a *tool*
that the learner should evaluate for themselves.

## Section 2 — Sole proprietor vs HK Ltd

The decision most learners are actually making.

| Dimension | Sole proprietor (HK BR only) | HK private Ltd (Cap. 622) |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal personality** | None — you and the business are the same | Separate legal person |
| **Liability** | Personal, unlimited | Limited to share capital and director duties |
| **Tax rate** | Personal salaries-tax style on profits | Profits tax: 8.25% on first HK$2M, 16.5% above |
| **Compliance burden** | BR renewal, IRD individual return | BR + CR (NNC1, NAR1, SCR) + IRD BIR51 + audited accounts |
| **Banking** | Personal-style account often acceptable | Business account required; KYC heavier |
| **Succession** | Ends with the proprietor | Shares transferable; company continues |
| **Perception** | "Freelancer" | "Company" — relevant for some clients and tenders |
| **Setup cost** | Low (BR fee only) | Moderate (CR fee + secretary + audit) |

The two-tier profits tax often makes the HK Ltd cheaper above modest profit levels, even after
audit costs. The simulation in Arc 2 walks the learner through the breakeven calculation for
their own numbers.

## Section 3 — The two-tier profits tax illustrator

Concrete numbers, deliberately rounded.

| Profit before tax (HK$) | Sole proprietor (rough) | HK Ltd two-tier |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 500,000 | salaries-tax bands apply | 41,250 (8.25% on full amount) |
| 1,000,000 | salaries-tax bands apply | 82,500 |
| 2,000,000 | salaries-tax bands apply | 165,000 (8.25% on first $2M) |
| 3,000,000 | salaries-tax bands apply | 165,000 + 165,000 = 330,000 |
| 5,000,000 | salaries-tax bands apply | 165,000 + 495,000 = 660,000 |

The illustrator the tutor walks through during this section computes:

- Profits tax under HK Ltd two-tier
- Estimated personal salaries tax if the same income were earned as a sole proprietor (with the
  caveat that allowances and bands change annually)
- Audit and secretary cost subtracted from the Ltd figure
- The breakeven profit at which Ltd becomes cheaper than sole proprietor for this learner

The output is an artifact the learner keeps.

> **Note.** The illustrator is educational. Real tax outcomes depend on allowances,
> deductions, the offshore-source position, and other facts. The product does not give tax
> advice and never auto-files anything to IRD.

## Section 4 — A day, a month, a year in an AI-augmented HK OPC

A walkthrough of *Lin Consulting Limited*, fictional, used throughout the simulation. Lin is 58,
recently retired from a HK pharmaceutical QA role, incorporated last quarter, has three clients.

### A typical day

- 09:00 — Tutor agent surfaces the calendar: one client call, two draft emails the agent prepared
  overnight, one categorization question Lin needs to confirm.
- 10:30 — Lin reviews and approves the email drafts. The agent sends them.
- 13:00 — A new invoice from a Singapore client arrives. The bookkeeping agent ingests the bank
  feed entry, proposes a category, asks Lin to confirm the offshore-source rationale (substance
  question — agent flags this as needing human judgement). Lin confirms in two clicks.
- 17:00 — End of day. Decision log shows seven agent actions, all approved.

### A typical month

- BR-renewal reminder (annual or 3-yearly cadence, agent-tracked).
- Provisional profits-tax sanity check.
- MPF self-contribution.
- Bookkeeping reconciliation against bank feed.
- Client retainer invoices drafted by the agent, sent after Lin approves.

### A typical year

- NAR1 (Annual Return) drafted by the compliance agent, filed by Lin (never autonomously).
- BIR51 (profits-tax return) prepared by the tax assistant from the year's books, filed by Lin
  after a human-CPA review.
- Audited financial statements — the agent prepares the working papers; a HK CPA signs.
- Annual review of the agent runbook itself: which tasks got delegated, which had to be redone,
  which need new guardrails.

The point is not magic. The point is that one human, with limited time, can credibly run all of
this — and that the structure scales to two or three service lines without hiring.

## Section 5 — Mastery check

A 5-7 exchange Socratic dialogue with the tutor. Sample prompts the tutor draws from:

1. "Why is the two-tier profits tax structurally favourable for a small HK OPC versus operating
   as a sole proprietor at the same revenue?"
2. "Name one thing in Lin's day that an agent must not do autonomously, and explain why."
3. "Your friend says: 'AI will run my company for me.' What is the strongest correction you would
   offer?"
4. "MPF replaces roughly what fraction of pre-retirement income for a HK professional? Why does
   that matter for the OPC pitch?"
5. "Lin's Singapore client. What is the agent allowed to do, and what must Lin personally
   decide?"

Failure on any prompt routes the learner to remediation in the relevant section, not to a penalty.

## Section 6 — Closing artifact

The learner leaves Arc 1 with:

- The tax illustrator output for their own assumed profit level.
- An "OPC fit" worksheet — five questions the tutor co-fills with the learner: expected profit
  range, primary client geography, comfort with audit, comfort with delegating to agents, and a
  succession preference.

These two artifacts seed Arc 2 (Incorporating in HK).
