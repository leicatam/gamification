# HK Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) — overview for the OPC tutor

Informational, not legal advice. Used by the tutor agent as canonical reference.

## What it is

Cap. 622 is Hong Kong's primary companies statute, in force since 3 March 2014, replacing the
1932 Companies Ordinance. It governs incorporation, governance, accounts, audit, and winding-up
of HK companies.

## Why it matters for the one-person Ltd

- **Single-person company is permitted.** A private company limited by shares may have one
  director (must be a natural person — at least one director cannot be a body corporate) and one
  shareholder, who may be the same person.
- **Company secretary required.** Must be a HK resident individual or a HK-registered body
  corporate. The sole director cannot also be the sole company secretary.
- **Registered office in HK required.** Service-provider addresses are common.
- **Articles of association.** Model articles are available; companies may adopt or modify them.
- **Annual return (NAR1).** Filed with the Companies Registry within 42 days of the anniversary of
  incorporation.
- **Significant Controllers Register (SCR).** Must be maintained at the registered office and
  produced on request.
- **Audit.** Mandatory audited financial statements for HK-incorporated companies, with limited
  exemptions.

## Filings the OPC will encounter

| Form | Trigger | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| NNC1 | Incorporation | One-time |
| NAR1 | Annual return | Annual |
| ND2A | Change of director particulars | As needed |
| NSC1 | Allotment of shares | As needed |
| NDR1 | Deregistration (solvent wind-up) | One-time |

(IRD filings — BR renewal, BIR51 profits-tax return — are separate, see `docs/hk/ird-overview.md`
when drafted.)

## What the OPC product does and does not do

- **Does** — explain Cap. 622 mechanics in plain language; help the learner draft NNC1, NAR1, and
  similar forms; track filing deadlines; produce working papers for an auditor.
- **Does not** — file forms with the Companies Registry on the learner's behalf; provide legal
  advice; sign audited financial statements; replace a HK-qualified company secretary or auditor.
