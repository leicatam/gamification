## 1. Originality — what survives if the identity mechanism is wrong?

The identity mechanism is presented throughout as a hypothesis, not a finding, so the contribution does not stand on it (§3.6 keeps three registers explicit: observations, candidate explanations, engineering responses). What survives are the observation-to-design decisions grounded in the first register: the dual-purpose mechanic itself — one COP signal driving gameplay and a silent clinical pipeline, specified to parameter level in §5.2A and Table 5.0/Game_States_and_Parameters.xlsx; the design principle that measurement and engagement may share hardware while using separated participant-facing pathways (§9.4); the credible-progress specification, which separates participation, condition-specific performance, learning and clinical change and forbids manufacturing a rising curve when the task changes (§7.5.2, Table 7.3); and the qualitative requirements mapping that makes each observed difficulty a reviewable design response (§5.5A, Table 5.3). These are useful to another engineer whatever explains the non-return, because they respond to the documented pattern — 70% early disengagement, zero observed re-engagement, and score gains coexisting with non-return (§5.5, archive G.2) — not to its explanation. §9.7 states this: adaptive learning is prior knowledge; the domain-specific integration and evaluation requirements are the contribution.

## 2. Ethics chronology — what did the August 2026 decision authorise?

The chronology is stated plainly in §3.4: application HSEARS20260805008 was submitted on 6 August 2026, after the field stages, and approved on 6 August 2026 by the Departmental Research Committee on behalf of the PolyU IRB, retrospectively, for the period 16 June 2024–30 October 2026 — covering the procedures as conducted and described, including the incomplete disclosure, the gatekeeper consent, and the retrospective written consent from sustained participants; the approval memo is archived (docs/thesis/ethics/HSEARS20260805008_Approval_Memo_as_received_2026-09-11.pdf). The documentary basis for using records of participants who did not sign is that those records are not self-administered research instruments: they are therapist-mediated, researcher-compiled observations gathered under the facility's routine care relationship and the gatekeeper arrangement, and the approved application described exactly this pathway (§3.4; Stage-2 ID register, Appendix G item G.2). I concede what retrospective approval cannot do: it does not convert the deployment into prospectively reviewed research, and the thesis treats that as a limitation examined in §8.7. If the Committee's view is required on the specific question of the unconsented participants' records, that is an institutional determination to be sought through the PI; the thesis meanwhile claims only that the procedures as conducted were described in, and covered by, the approved application.

## 3. Response provenance — how did a conversation become a data value?

For a low-engagement participant, the satisfaction value comes from the after-game paper questionnaire that every participant completed at the end of play (Appendix C; §3.4); the symptom and symptom-change values come from therapists speaking with players in ordinary facility practice and relaying observations to me on near-daily visits or calls, which I recorded in field notes and attributed to individuals when compiling the dataset (§3.4). Attribution was checked against the deployment logbook's own column provenance in the Stage-2 ID register received 11 September 2026 (Appendix G, item G.2), which is what reconciled the two earlier accounts: satisfaction is participant-reported, symptoms are therapist-observed. The remaining uncertainty is stated in §5.5: the therapists kept no written per-participant records, so a single, unblinded compiler performed the individual attribution, and recall and expectancy effects cannot be excluded. For that reason the satisfaction–engagement association (Fisher's exact p = 0.013) is read as an observed association in compiled field data, not an instrumented self-report result, and the confirmatory study replaces this pathway with self-administered instruments at pre-specified times (§5.5). The engagement classification itself does not depend on these mediated variables — walking away was directly observed behaviour.

## 4. Failure, cycle, session, opportunity to return — definitions and contemporaneity

In Stage 2, a completed game cycle is one attended play session at the station under the five-attempt rule; the three-or-fewer-cycles threshold defining low engagement is an observation-derived, retrospectively formalised cut, matching where walk-aways clustered (§5.5). A failure is an in-game failure event — obstacle hits, falls, low-scoring runs — as recorded in my field notes (§5.6), and the opportunity to return was the station's continued availability across the eight-week window. Contemporaneously recorded were cycle counts and the pre/post index values (archive G.2) and my near-daily field notes; the archive holds no session timestamps, which is why the survival analysis and hazard ratio of earlier drafts were withdrawn (§5.5). In Stage 3 the definitions are stated in §6.4 — initial dropout (incomplete first session or no second session in window), return (a subsequently logged completed session), observed non-return, and end-of-window retention counting returned dropouts as retained — and the contemporaneous record is the HTML build's logged CSV returned by e-mail, compiled and recomputed in the reconciliation logbook (Appendix G, item G.3, with G.5 dating distributions and returns). Stage 1 events are retrospective self-report only (§4.2), and no contemporaneity is claimed for them.

## 5. Measurement — what index, and what survives the task-context objection?

The index is the InBody FRA composite from the FRA510S COP pathway, on which higher is better, calibrated near a population average of 70 and positively correlated with the Berg Balance Scale and SPPB (§5.5; §4.1A; InBody Co., Ltd., 2022); the 'before' value is a standard-mode assessment at first attendance and the 'after' is the game-mode index from the final play, both generated automatically by the same measurement engine from the same COP pathway (§5.5). I do not defend the pairing as a clean measurement design — §5.5 states plainly that the two values were produced under different task contexts, so the recorded change contains any effect of the measurement context itself, that post-measurement times vary by participant, and that changes of this size lie within reported test-retest variability (Kim et al., 2018). If the task-context difference explains the change, the thesis's conclusions survive intact, because no clinical or learning claim rests on the index: what carries weight is the engagement record — 70% early disengagement, zero observed re-engagement, and recorded score direction diverging from return in five of eight single-session exits (§5.5, §5.6). §5.5 even notes that a frame-driven component of the change would run in the direction of the programme's own frame-sensitivity account, and explicitly declines to treat it as evidence for it.

## 6. Clinical framing — what did Stage-2 participants believe, see, and hear?

Participants were residents who knew the FRA as part of the house's provision; they were invited by the facility, through therapists they knew, to try the gamified system as a voluntary facility activity, and were not told during the deployment that play formed part of a research project (§3.4). What they saw was a coupled clinical-game context: the known assessment station, a standard-mode FRA assessment at first attendance, an InBody scan, clinical pass/fail language and repeated use of the term "assessment" (§6.3, §5.5), while the play screen itself carried no clinical language and the comprehensive score stayed on the clinician-side terminal (§5.2A, §5.3.3). So clinical coupling at the participant level is established by the setting and procedure — every participant passed through the assessment frame before and around play — but I concede that no participant-level measure of perceived coupling was collected in Stage 2; the frame-recognition manipulation check exists only in Stage 3 (§6.5). That absence is exactly why §6.7 keeps clinical framing at candidate level and why the harmonised within-cohort comparison in §8.8 carries a perceived-frame measure.

## 7. The Stage-3 contrast — separating framing from everything else that changed

I cannot separate them within the present data, and the thesis says so: the decoupling package changed platform, input device, setting, supervision, recruitment and cohort alongside participant-facing clinical cues, and dispersed home play also removed the visible-dropout social channel (§6.3, §6.9, §8.5.8; §3.5 states the between-cohort design is not equivalent to randomisation). The calibrated claim is deliberately associational: participant-facing decoupling was associated with substantially greater post-failure return in a separate cohort — 15 of 23 dropouts returning, 10 of 16 in the 65–80 band against 0 of 21 in Stage 2 — and the pattern warrants direct causal testing (§6.5, §6.7). The smallest informative comparison is specified in §8.8: one community sample, randomised to coupled versus participant-facing decoupled conditions, holding constant hardware, software, level structure, input modality, session length, staff scripts, eligibility and follow-up, with observed return in a pre-specified window as the primary outcome. Input modality gets its own sentence because §8.5.8 concedes it as a viable rival that the proposed comparison holds constant, and a separately designed modality study would be needed to estimate embodiment effects.

## 8. FIAS versus ordinary discontinuance and rational consequence-avoidance

Observations that would make me prefer the rivals are identified in §8.5.9–§8.5.10: if satisfaction, burden, boredom or expected-benefit measures predicted non-return and identity-relevant appraisals added nothing, ordinary discontinuance wins (Eysenbach, 2005; Bhattacherjee, 2001); and if measured perception of the verdict's practical consequences — prescribed training and recommendations against walking alone, documented as the station's operating practice in §4.1A — carried the association, rational consequence-avoidance wins, a rival the thesis concedes as live, unmeasured, and only partly displaceable because it shares the coupling structure. The one recorded point that currently resists the boredom and low-relevance accounts is the field-note sequence in §5.6: players who acknowledged the system's benefits and were recording index gains gave up after recurring in-game failures. Incremental value is tested without criterion contamination by the three-tier nested architecture of §6.10: FIAS appraisals measured before the prediction interval, with cycle count, return status and any outcome-defining element excluded from the predictor score (a rule also fixed in Appendix B), judged by likelihood-ratio, calibration, discrimination and decision-curve criteria against demographics and STAM. That comparison is specified, not executed — §6.10 opens by saying so — and the eight Stage-3 non-return events are insufficient for it, so it is reserved for a future dataset.

## 9. The artefact — which adaptive decisions were actually made, and what trace exists?

In the deployed Stage-2 build, the adaptive decisions were Layer-2 difficulty adjustments — skier speed, coin interval and group size, obstacle density — made every five minutes by a LangChain-orchestrated model call over the recorded endpoint, on a prompt anchored to recent success rate (raise above 80%, lower below 40%), on top of the static rule table of §5.2A (age band × self-reported balance multipliers from the Assessment screen); in Stage 3, no model call operated in any session — the ported HTML build ran the rule-based fallback agent only, because a distributed file carries no credentials (§5.3.4–§5.3.5; Appendix E). Bounded behaviour is specified in Appendix E.3: graceful degradation to the rule heuristic when the endpoint is unavailable, no identifiers off-device, all AI calls logged, and a manual staff override; input-side bounds are the deadzones and the 1.5× speed clamp of §5.2A. The available trace is the Layer-3 per-session JSON log — COP samples, level transitions, success/failure events, AI-call telemetry and timestamps (Appendix E.1) — plus the prompt-template archive with versions and settings (Appendix G, item G.7). I concede the honest gap: which session logs were actually retained is still being verified (the G.2 entry binds telemetry "where retained"), and the build-lineage matrix G.0A — which build, which adaptation mechanism, which participants — is an open author-records item; until those rows are filled, I demonstrate the mechanism from the archived specification and prompt archive rather than claiming a complete per-session audit trail.

## 10. Improvement — easier play versus learning, and the right to stop

The system distinguishes them by refusing to let one score stand for four different things: §7.5.2 requires participation, condition-specific performance, learning and clinical change to be reported separately, with every performance observation retaining task version, difficulty, input modality and assistance, and with brief reference checks under predefined comparable conditions as the basis for change claims — repeated success in the adaptive task alone is insufficient (Soderstrom & Bjork, 2015). The worked example is explicit: a participant completing more of a slower, assisted task is reported as exactly that, and only a later reference check at reduced assistance licenses a reduced-support claim. An engineer knows a revision improved the service through the second loop of §9.12: a pre-stated outcome, a defensible comparison, and — when the redesign changes the task — a marked phase boundary and preserved history rather than a fresh baseline that conceals earlier difficulty; if outcomes worsen or feedback misleads, the revision is reversed. The participant's choice to stop is protected by design scope: §8.7.3 targets the intervention at the gap between stated intent and behaviour, not at people who have decided the technology is not for them, and requires staff training that coaching must never override a clearly stated preference for non-engagement, with the paternalism risk separately flagged in §8.7.4. None of this is claimed as demonstrated — the complete framework and progress display remain untested as a package (§9.5), which is precisely why they are written as testable specifications.
