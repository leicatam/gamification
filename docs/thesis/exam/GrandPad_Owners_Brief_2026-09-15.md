## 2. What GrandPad is — five facts to know cold

1. A purpose-built **tablet computer** for older adults from GrandPad, Inc.
   (USA): radically simplified interface, video calls, photos, music, simple
   games. Subscription on the order of US$500/year, cellular built in — no
   home broadband or set-up needed. ("Grand" as in grandparent — it is a
   tablet in the hands; the InBody panel is a plate under the feet.)
2. Published evaluations report high ease of use and connectedness among
   predominantly **homebound** users where the device filled an access gap —
   and weaker adoption where mainstream alternatives already existed.
3. Its **enterprise** line (Daily Connect, announced 3 June 2020) adds
   connected health devices — vital-signs monitor (blood pressure, pulse
   oximetry, temperature, heart rate, respiration), weight scale, wearable —
   whose readings are transmitted **to remote caregivers** when values fall
   outside the person's normal range.
4. Its Element Care case (13 Oct 2021 release) describes telehealth and
   remote patient monitoring **inside a supervised PACE care programme**.
5. In every documented configuration: the user is never confronted with an
   assessment verdict, and **the engagement activity is never itself the
   measurement**. That is the exact mirror image of the Stage-2 FRA, where
   the game WAS the measurement and the player knew it.

## 3. What the thesis claims about it — and deliberately does not

CLAIMS: sustained engagement is documented only in configurations where no
verdict reaches the user; the enterprise configuration adds monitoring
functions, identified from vendor material "that describes functions rather
than outcomes"; no documented product delivers its measurement to the user
while remaining bearable and returnable-to — that empty quadrant is the
design target.

DOES NOT CLAIM: that GrandPad "carries no clinical measurement" (the old,
incorrect dichotomy — corrected in V42 after the examiner checked the vendor
materials); that GrandPad solves or fails the FRA problem; anything about
GrandPad's effectiveness (functions only, per the reference notes).

## 4. Likely questions, honest answers

**"Did you use GrandPad in your research?"**
No. It is a market comparator cited from vendor materials and published
evaluations, used to position the design space in §7.9. My empirical work is
the FRA deployments of Stages 1–3.

**"But GrandPad does collect clinical data — doesn't that break your
comparison?"**
Yes, its enterprise line collects vitals — and it routes the readings to the
caregiver. The user never faces a verdict, and the games are never the
measurement. My programme is about what happens when the play IS the
measurement and the user knows it: that is the exposure Stage 2 had, Stage 3
removed, and Stage 3B re-introduces with adaptive support. Figure 8.1's axis
is user-facing clinical assessment for exactly this reason.

**"Why compare a fall-risk assessment station to a consumer tablet?"**
Because the comparison marks the boundary of the documented design space:
engagement is solved where assessment is absent from the user's experience;
assessment with sustained voluntary engagement has no documented exemplar.
An EngD contribution is positioned against practice, and that empty quadrant
is the practice gap the adaptive-learning framework targets.

**"Who identified this comparator?"** (if asked)
It came out of the AI-assisted drafting process the thesis discloses; I then
verified the product facts against GrandPad's own published materials — the
2020 connected-device announcement and the 2021 PACE release are cited with
access dates — and I take responsibility for the comparison as it stands.

## 5. The one-breath answer

"GrandPad is a consumer tablet for older adults — engagement without any
user-facing assessment. My FRA is the opposite pole — assessment delivered
as a verdict, and non-return followed. The design target of this thesis is
the empty quadrant between them: a technology that measures the user, tells
the user, and is still returned to voluntarily. Nothing documented occupies
it yet."
