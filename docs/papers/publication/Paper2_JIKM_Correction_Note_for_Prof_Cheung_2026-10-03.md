# Paper 2 (JIKM, under review, JIKMS2602466) — correction note for Prof. Cheung

Prepared 3 October 2026 from the author's account of the same date. **Not sent to the journal.** For Prof. Cheung's decision on whether to notify the editor now or hold the corrections for the revision round.

## The point
The submitted manuscript describes the skiing game as reading the InBody panel's centre-of-pressure (COP) stream and using it as its control input, with the clinical index computed from the same stream. On the author's account (3 October 2026), confirmed in thesis V78:
- the InBody FRA510S exposes no application programming interface, and the attempt to integrate the game with it failed on that point;
- the game was steered by a separate, generic force-sensing-resistor pressure mat placed on top of the FRA footplate, used as a left/right controller in the manner of a keyboard's arrow keys; the mat's pressure values were never measured or recorded;
- the InBody software measured balance from the footplate beneath the mat, in parallel; the two systems kept separate data sets, joined only by the therapist's hand record at the end of each game; the first version of the game had been tested by keyboard;
- the game's electronic telemetry and AI-call logs were not kept.
The thesis (V78) states this throughout. The paper's Methods, three figure captions and the knowledge-management discussion say the opposite, and a reviewer who holds thesis and paper side by side will see the conflict.

## Secondary point
The paper's Layer-3 description ("a language-model service … reviews performance at five-minute intervals") is stated as operating. The thesis (V68 onward) states it "as specified", with no operational claim, because no build file, prompt archive or call log for the deployed build survives. The same qualification would protect the paper.
